Every sales leader I talk to has the same mandate right now: get AI into the sales process. Usually by next quarter.

So they buy an AI SDR or a research agent.

Six weeks later, reps have gone back to their own habits, and someone asks whether the tool was a waste of money.

**Usually, nobody wrote down the process the tool was supposed to automate.**

## Your process lives in people's heads

Ask five reps how they work a new inbound lead. You'll get five answers.

One checks LinkedIn first. Another skips research entirely if the title looks junior.

Your top performer does something nobody else does, and she couldn't explain it if you asked.

That works while humans do the work, because humans fill in what's missing with judgment.

Software can't. Point an AI at five versions of a process and you get a sixth that matches none of them.

## What "documented" actually means

A documented process answers these questions for every step.

1. **What triggers this step?** A lead assigned, a reply received, a meeting no-show.
2. **What decision gets made here, and on what criteria?** Write criteria a stranger could check, like "200+ employees, uses a contact center platform, visited pricing twice."
3. **What happens on each branch?** Every yes and every no needs a next step.

Answer those for every step and you have a decision tree, which a machine can follow.

## Then draw the human line

Once the steps are on paper, give each one a verdict.

| Verdict | What happens | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Automate** | Software does it end to end. No review. | Enrichment, routing, logging activity |
| **Assist** | Software drafts, a human approves. | First-touch emails, call prep briefs |
| **Human** | A person owns it. Software stays out. | Discovery calls, pricing exceptions |

Base each verdict on what a mistake would cost.

A bad enrichment field costs nothing. A tone-deaf email to a Fortune 500 VP costs the account.

## Why this order matters

Skip the documentation and you automate a mess faster.

Do it first, and tool demos turn into a checklist. You know exactly which steps you're buying software for.

Adoption goes up too. Reps trust a system when they can see the rules it follows, especially rules they helped write.

> An AI-native team has a process clear enough for a machine to run the parts it should.

## Where to start

Pick one workflow that eats your team's week. Inbound lead handling is usually the best candidate because it's frequent, measurable, and close to pipeline.

Sit with two reps and write down every step and every exception.

Then draw the tree, and mark which steps software should handle.

That one page will tell you more about where AI belongs in your sales process than any vendor demo.
