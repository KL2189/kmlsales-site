"Human in the loop" is a phrase everyone agrees with and nobody defines. Which step, and which human?

If you can't answer that for each step, reps either approve whatever the AI produces or ignore it entirely.

## Two questions for every step

**1. What does a mistake cost?**

Assume AI gets it wrong sometimes, and ask what happens next.

A wrong industry tag on a lead record costs nothing, because someone fixes it later.

A wrong company name in a first-touch email to a target account costs you the account, and probably a screenshot in someone's Slack.

**2. Can the mistake be undone?**

A lead routed to the wrong rep gets fixed in minutes. A sent email or a discount quoted on a call stays sent.

## Turn the answers into a verdict

| Mistake cost | Can it be undone? | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Low | Yes | **Automate** |
| Medium | No | **Assist** (AI drafts, a human approves) |
| High | No | **Human** (software stays out) |

Run your process through this and a pattern shows up fast.

Routing and logging land in automate, and first drafts land in assist.

Anything involving pricing or a strategic account stays human.

## Assist is where most of the value is

The automate column gets the attention because it sounds like headcount savings. The bigger win is usually assist.

An SDR who spends 40 minutes researching an account and writing a first email can review an AI-drafted version in five.

The rep gets most of the day back for real conversations with buyers.

Assist only works if the review is real.

If approving a draft is one click with no friction, it'll get approved without being read.

## Move the line on purpose

You can move the line later.

As a step proves itself, with low error rates and no surprises, promote it from assist to automate. If something goes wrong, pull it back.

Whoever moves it should write down why.

> Put a human exactly where a human earns their keep, and let software handle the rest.
